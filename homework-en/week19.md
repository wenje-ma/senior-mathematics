> **Definition 3.1.1 (Function)**
> 
> 设 $A,B$ 是非空的实数集, 如果对于集合 $A$ 中的任意一个数 $x$, 按照某种确定的对应关系 $f$, 在集合 $B$ 中都有唯一确定的数 $y$ 和它对应, 就称 $f:A\to{B}$ 为从集合 $A$ 到集合 $B$ 的一个函数, 记作 $y=f\left(x\right)$, $x\in{A}$.

> **Definition 3.1.2 (Domain and Range)**
> 
> 对于函数 $y=f\left(x\right)$, $x\in{A}$, 其中 $x$ 叫做自变量, $x$ 的取值范围 $A$ 叫做函数的定义域, 与 $x$ 的值相对应的 $y$ 值叫做函数值, 函数值的集合 $\left\{f\left(x\right)\mid{x}\in{A}\right\}$ 叫做函数的值域.

> **Definition 3.1.5 (Piecewise Function)**
> 
> 在函数的定义域内, 对于自变量 $x$ 的不同取值范围, 有着不同的对应关系, 这样的函数通常叫做分段函数.

> **Definition 3.2.1 (Monotonically Increasing)**
> 
> 设函数 $f\left(x\right)$ 的定义域为 $I$, 区间 $D\subseteq{I}$: 如果 $\forall{x}_1,x_2\in{D}$, 当 $x_1<{}x_2$ 时, 都有 $f\left(x_1\right)<{}f\left(x_2\right)$, 那么就称函数 $f\left(x\right)$ 在区间 $D$ 上单调递增.

> **Definition 3.2.2 (Monotonically Decreasing)**
> 
> 设函数 $f\left(x\right)$ 的定义域为 $I$, 区间 $D\subseteq{I}$: 如果 $\forall{x}_1,x_2\in{D}$, 当 $x_1<{}x_2$ 时, 都有 $f\left(x_1\right)>{}f\left(x_2\right)$, 那么就称函数 $f\left(x\right)$ 在区间 $D$ 上单调递减.

> **Definition 3.2.3 (Maximum Value)**
> 
> 设函数 $y=f\left(x\right)$ 的定义域为 $I$, 如果存在实数 $M$ 满足: $\forall{x}\in{I}$, 都有 $f\left(x\right)\le{M}$; $\exists{x}_0\in{I}$, 使得 $f\left(x_0\right)=M$. 则称 $M$ 是函数 $y=f\left(x\right)$ 的最大值.

> **Definition 3.2.5 (Even Function)**
> 
> 设函数 $f\left(x\right)$ 的定义域为 $I$, 如果 $\forall{x}\in{I}$, 都有 $-x\in{I}$, 且 $f\left(-x\right)=f\left(x\right)$, 那么函数 $f\left(x\right)$ 就叫做偶函数.

> **Definition 3.2.6 (Odd Function)**
> 
> 设函数 $f\left(x\right)$ 的定义域为 $I$, 如果 $\forall{x}\in{I}$, 都有 $-x\in{I}$, 且 $f\left(-x\right)=-f\left(x\right)$, 那么函数 $f\left(x\right)$ 就叫做奇函数.

> **Definition 3.3.1 (Power Function)**
> 
> 一般地, 函数 $y=x^\alpha$ 叫做幂函数, 其中 $x$ 是自变量, $\alpha$ 是常数.

> **Definition 4.1.1 (n 次方根)**
> 
> 一般地, 如果 x^n = a, 那么 x 叫做 a 的 n 次方根, 其中 n > 1, 且 n 是正整数.

> **Definition 4.4.1 (对数函数)**
> 
> 一般地, 函数 $y=\log_ax$ ($a>0$, 且 $a\neq1$) 叫做对数函数, 其中 $x$ 是自变量, 定义域是 $\left(0,+\infty\right)$.

## Problem 1

(Review Reference Problem 2, Review and Consolidation, Problem 3)

(1) Among sectors with fixed area $S$, for what radius is the perimeter of the sector minimized?

(2) Among sectors with fixed perimeter $P$, for what radius is the area of the sector maximized?

### Solution to Problem 1

By **Common Conclusion 2.2.2** (Fixed Product Minimizes Sum, Fixed Sum Maximizes Product), let the radius of the sector be $R$ and the arc length be $l$. The sector area is $\frac{1}{2}Rl$, and the perimeter is $2R+l$.

(1) The area $S=\frac{1}{2}Rl$ is fixed, i.e. $Rl=2S$. The perimeter $2R+l\ge2\sqrt{2Rl}=2\sqrt{4S}=4\sqrt{S}$, with equality if and only if $2R=l$. At this time $Rl=2R^2=2S$, giving $R=\sqrt{S}$. Hence the perimeter is minimized when the radius is $\sqrt{S}$.

(2) The perimeter $P=2R+l$ is fixed. Let $u=2R$, then $u+l=P$, and the area $\frac{1}{2}Rl=\frac{1}{4}ul\le\frac{1}{4}\cdot\left(\frac{u+l}{2}\right)^2=\frac{P^2}{16}$, with equality if and only if $u=l$. At this time $2R=l=\frac{P}{2}$, giving $R=\frac{P}{4}$. Hence the area is maximized when the radius is $\frac{P}{4}$, and the maximum value is $\frac{P^2}{16}$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 2

(Review Reference Problem 2, Review and Consolidation, Problem 4) Find the solution set of the following inequalities.

(1) $14-4x^2\ge{x}$;

(2) $x^2-14x+45\le0$;

(3) $x^2+6x+10>0$;

(4) $x\left(x+2\right)>{}x\left(3-x\right)+1$.

### Solution to Problem 2

By **Common Conclusion 2.3.3** (Solution Set of a Quadratic Inequality in One Variable), solve each one.

(1) Rearranging gives $4x^2+x-14\le0$. The roots of $4x^2+x-14=0$ are $x=-2,\frac{7}{4}$, hence the solution set is $\left\{x\mid-2\le{x}\le\frac{7}{4}\right\}$.

(2) $\left(x-5\right)\left(x-9\right)\le0$, roots $x=5,9$, hence the solution set is $\left\{x\mid5\le{x}\le9\right\}$.

(3) $\Delta=36-40=-4<0$, opening upward, hence the solution set is $\mathbb{R}$.

(4) Expanding and rearranging gives $2x^2-x-1>0$, i.e. $\left(2x+1\right)\left(x-1\right)>0$, roots $x=-\frac{1}{2},1$, hence the solution set is $\left\{x\mid{x}<-\frac{1}{2}$ or $x>1\right\}$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 3

(Review Reference Problem 2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 5) If $a,b>0$ and $ab=a+b+3$, find the range of $ab$.

### Solution to Problem 3

By **Theorem 2.2.1 (Basic Inequality)**, $a+b\ge2\sqrt{ab}$.

From $ab=a+b+3$, we get $ab\ge2\sqrt{ab}+3$. Let $t=\sqrt{ab}>0$, then

$$
t^2\ge2t+3,
$$

i.e. $\left(t-3\right)\left(t+1\right)\ge0$. Since $t>0$, we have $t\ge3$, and hence $ab=t^2\ge9$. Equality holds if and only if $a=b=3$. Hence the range of $ab$ is $\left\{x\mid{x}\ge9\right\}$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 4

(Review Reference Problem 2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 6) For what value of $k$ does the quadratic inequality $2kx^2+kx-\frac{3}{8}<0$ hold for all real numbers $x$?

### Solution to Problem 4

By **Common Conclusion 2.3.3** (Solution Set of a Quadratic Inequality in One Variable), $ax^2+bx+c<0$ holding for all real numbers requires opening downward ($a<0$) and having no real roots ($\Delta<0$).

First, $2k<0$, i.e. $k<0$.

Also require $\Delta=k^2-4\cdot2k\cdot\left(-\frac{3}{8}\right)=k^2+3k<0$, i.e. $k\left(k+3\right)<0$, solving to $-3<{}k<0$.

Intersecting with $k<0$ gives $-3<{}k<0$. Hence when $-3<{}k<0$, the inequality holds for all real numbers $x$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 5

(Review Reference Problem 2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 7) It is generally believed that in a residential building the window area must be smaller than the floor area, but the ratio of window area to floor area should be no less than $10\%$; and the larger this ratio, the better the lighting.

(1) If the total of the window area and floor area of an apartment is $220\,\mathrm{m}^2$, what is the minimum window area of this apartment in square meters?

(2) If the same window area and floor area are added simultaneously, does the apartment's lighting get better or worse?

### Solution to Problem 5

Let the window area be $w$ and the floor area be $f$, then $w<{}f$ and $\frac{w}{f}\ge0.1$.

(1) From $w+f=220$, i.e. $f=220-w$. From $\frac{w}{f}\ge0.1$, we get $w\ge0.1\left(220-w\right)$, i.e. $1.1w\ge22$, hence $w\ge20$. Hence the window area is at least $20\,\mathrm{m}^2$.

(2) Let the window and floor areas both increase by $m>0$. By **Basic Fact 2.1.1** (Order Relations of Real Numbers), compare the new ratio with the old:

$$
\frac{w+m}{f+m}-\frac{w}{f}=\frac{f\left(w+m\right)-w\left(f+m\right)}{f\left(f+m\right)}=\frac{m\left(f-w\right)}{f\left(f+m\right)}.
$$

Since $f>{}w$, the numerator is positive, hence the difference is positive, i.e. the new ratio is larger. Hence after adding the same area to both, the lighting improves. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 6

(Review Reference Problem 2, Comprehensive Application, Problem 8) There is a correspondence between equalities and inequalities: one can obtain a corresponding inequality proposition simply by changing the equals sign in an equality proposition to an inequality sign. Please use analogy to explore the corresponding properties of equalities and inequalities, and following the table below list as many corresponding propositions as you can; indicate whether the listed corresponding inequality propositions are correct, and explain why.

### Solution to Problem 6

By **Property 2.1.5** (Additivity), **Property 2.1.6** (Multiplicativity) and **Basic Fact 2.1.1** (Order Relations of Real Numbers), list analogously and judge.

(1) If $x=y$, then $x+z=y+z$; corresponding: if $x>{}y$, then $x+z>{}y+z$. Correct, i.e. **Property 2.1.5** (Additivity).

(2) If $x=y$, then $xz=yz$; corresponding: if $x>{}y$, then $xz>{}yz$. Incorrect; when $z<0$ the inequality sign reverses.

(3) If $x=y$, then $x^3=y^3$; corresponding: if $x>{}y$, then $x^3>{}y^3$. Correct, since $x^3-y^3=\left(x-y\right)\left(x^2+xy+y^2\right)>0$.

(4) If $x=y$, then $x^2=y^2$; corresponding: if $x>{}y$, then $x^2>{}y^2$. Incorrect; e.g. $1>-2$ but $1<4$; it holds only when the two numbers have the same sign.

(5) If $x=y$, $z\neq0$, then $\frac{x}{z}=\frac{y}{z}$; corresponding: if $x>{}y$, then $\frac{x}{z}>\frac{y}{z}$. Correct when $z>0$, incorrect when $z<0$.

It can be seen that propositions analogous to addition are mostly correct; when analogous to multiplication and division, a negative multiplier or divisor reverses the inequality sign, so an additional condition is needed. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 7

(Review Reference Problem 2, Extension and Exploration, Problem 9) As shown in the figure, a residential community wants to build an octagonal leisure area; its main floor plan is a cross-shaped region of area $200\,\mathrm{m}^2$ formed by two identical rectangles $ABCD$ and $EFGH$. It is planned to build a flower bed on the square $MNPQ$, costing $4200$ yuan$/\mathrm{m}^2$; to pave granite flooring on the four identical rectangles (the shaded parts in the figure), costing $210$ yuan$/\mathrm{m}^2$; and to lay lawn on the four empty corners (the four triangles in the figure), costing $80$ yuan$/\mathrm{m}^2$. Let the total cost be $S$ (unit: yuan) and the length $AD$ be $x$ (unit: $\mathrm{m}$). For what value of $x$ is $S$ minimized? And find this minimum value.

The figure: a plan of an octagonal cross-shaped (plus-sign) leisure area, formed by two congruent rectangles $ABCD$ and $EFGH$ overlapping orthogonally; the central overlap is the square $MNPQ$ (flower bed); from the central square a rectangle extends in each of the four directions up, down, left, and right (the shaded regions, paved with granite), and between the four outer ends of the cross and the enclosing square lie four corner triangles (lawn); the length $AD$ is denoted by $x$.

### Solution to Problem 7

By **Common Conclusion 2.2.2** (Fixed Product Minimizes Sum, Fixed Sum Maximizes Product), let the cross be formed by two congruent rectangles overlapping; the central square $MNPQ$ has side $x$, and each rectangle has length $x+2t$, where $t$ is half the protruding part. The cross area is $2x\left(x+2t\right)-x^2=x^2+4xt=200$.

The flower bed area is $x^2$; the four shaded rectangles total $4\cdot{x}t$; the four empty corner triangles total $4\cdot\frac{1}{2}t^2=2t^2$. The total cost is

$$
S=4200x^2+210\cdot4xt+80\cdot2t^2=4200x^2+840xt+160t^2.
$$

From $x^2+4xt=200$ we get $xt=\frac{200-x^2}{4}$. Substituting gives

$$
S=4200x^2+840\cdot\frac{200-x^2}{4}+160t^2=4000x^2+38000+\frac{400000}{x^2}.
$$

By **Theorem 2.2.1 (Basic Inequality)**,

$$
4000x^2+\frac{400000}{x^2}\ge2\sqrt{4000x^2\cdot\frac{400000}{x^2}}=80000,
$$

with equality if and only if $4000x^2=\frac{400000}{x^2}$, i.e. $x^2=10$, $x=\sqrt{10}$. At this time $S=80000+38000=118000$ yuan. Hence when $x=\sqrt{10}\,\mathrm{m}$ the total cost is minimized, and the minimum is $118000$ yuan. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 8

(Review Reference Problem 2, Extension and Exploration, Problem 10) When buying the same item, one may use two different strategies. The first is: regardless of rises or falls in the item's price, each time buy a fixed quantity of the item. The second is: regardless of rises or falls in the item's price, each time spend a fixed amount on the item. Which shopping method is more economical? Can you generalize the conclusion obtained?

### Solution to Problem 8

By **Theorem 2.2.1 (Basic Inequality)**, let the two prices be $p_1,p_2>0$, and compare the average price of the two strategies.

First strategy: each time buy a fixed quantity $q$; in total spend $q\left(p_1+p_2\right)$, buy $2q$, and the average price is $\frac{p_1+p_2}{2}$ (the arithmetic mean).

Second strategy: each time spend a fixed amount $m$; in total buy $\frac{m}{p_1}+\frac{m}{p_2}$, spend $2m$, and the average price is

$$
\frac{2m}{\frac{m}{p_1}+\frac{m}{p_2}}=\frac{2p_1p_2}{p_1+p_2}
$$

(the harmonic mean).

By **Theorem 2.2.1 (Basic Inequality)**, $\frac{2p_1p_2}{p_1+p_2}\le\frac{p_1+p_2}{2}$, with equality if and only if $p_1=p_2$. Hence the second strategy (spending a fixed amount each time) has a lower average price and is more economical.

Generalization: when prices fluctuate, the strategy of investing a fixed amount each time is superior to the strategy of buying a fixed quantity each time, and its average price is never higher than the latter. $\blacksquare$

### Solution to Problem 12

## Problem 13

(Review Reference Problem 3, Review and Consolidation, Problem 5) Given that the graph of the power function $y=f\left(x\right)$ passes through the point $\left(2,\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\right)$, find the analytic expression of this function, sketch its graph, and determine its odd-even property and monotonicity.

The graph: in the rectangular coordinate plane, the graph of the power function $y=x^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$ lies only in the first quadrant (domain $\left(0,+\infty\right)$); it is a monotonically decreasing curve that falls from $+\infty$ as $x\to0^+$ as $x$ increases, gradually approaching both coordinate axes as asymptotes, and passes through the point $\left(2,\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\right)$.

### Solution to Problem 13

By **Definition 3.3.1 (Power Function)**, let $f\left(x\right)=x^\alpha$ and substitute the given point.

The domain is $\left(0,+\infty\right)$. The domain is not symmetric about the origin, hence it is neither an odd nor an even function.

By **Definition 3.2.2 (Monotonic Decrease and Decreasing Function)**, on $\left(0,+\infty\right)$ as $x$ increases, $\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$ decreases, hence it is monotonically decreasing. The graph lies in the first quadrant, with the two coordinate axes as asymptotes. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 14

(Review Reference Problem 3, Review and Consolidation, Problem 6) A company producing a certain electronic instrument has a fixed cost of $20000$ yuan, and each instrument produced requires an additional investment of $100$ yuan. The total revenue $R$ (unit: yuan) as a function of the monthly output $x$ (unit: instruments) satisfies:

$$
R=\begin{cases}400x-\frac{1}{2}x^2,&0\le{x}\le400,\\80000,&x>400.\end{cases}
$$

(1) Express the profit $P$ (unit: yuan) as a function of the monthly output $x$;

(2) For what monthly output does the company obtain the maximum profit? What is the maximum profit in yuan? (Total revenue $=$ total cost $+$ profit)

### Solution to Problem 14

By **Definition 3.2.3 (Maximum Value)** and **Definition 3.1.5 (Piecewise Function)**.

(1) The total cost is $20000+100x$. The profit is $P=R-\left(20000+100x\right)$.

When $0\le{x}\le400$, $P=400x-\frac{1}{2}x^2-20000-100x=-\frac{1}{2}x^2+300x-20000$.

When $x>400$, $P=80000-20000-100x=60000-100x$.

Hence

$$
P=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2}x^2+300x-20000,&0\le{x}\le400,\\60000-100x,&x>400.\end{cases}
$$

(2) When $0\le{x}\le400$, $P=-\frac{1}{2}\left(x-300\right)^2+25000$, attaining the maximum $25000$ at $x=300$. When $x>400$, $P=60000-100x<60000-40000=20000<25000$. Hence the profit is maximized when the monthly output is $300$ instruments, and the maximum profit is $25000$ yuan. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 16

(Review Reference Problem 3, Comprehensive Application, Problem 8) Prove:

(1) If $f\left(x\right)=ax+b$, then $f\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}\right)=\frac{f\left(x_1\right)+f\left(x_2\right)}{2}$;

(2) If $g\left(x\right)=x^2+ax+b$, then $g\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}\right)\le\frac{g\left(x_1\right)+g\left(x_2\right)}{2}$.

(1) Left side: $f\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}\right)=a\cdot\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}+b=\frac{ax_1+ax_2}{2}+b$.

Right side: $\frac{f\left(x_1\right)+f\left(x_2\right)}{2}=\frac{\left(ax_1+b\right)+\left(ax_2+b\right)}{2}=\frac{ax_1+ax_2}{2}+b$.

The left side equals the right side, hence the equality holds.

(2) Right side minus left side:

$$
\frac{g\left(x_1\right)+g\left(x_2\right)}{2}-g\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}\right)=\frac{x_1^2+x_2^2}{2}-\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}\right)^2=\frac{2x_1^2+2x_2^2-x_1^2-2x_1x_2-x_2^2}{4}=\frac{\left(x_1-x_2\right)^2}{4}\ge0.
$$

Hence $g\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}\right)\le\frac{g\left(x_1\right)+g\left(x_2\right)}{2}$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 17

(Review Reference Problem 3, Comprehensive Application, Problem 9) (1) Given that an odd function $f\left(x\right)$ is monotonically decreasing on $\left[a,b\right]$, is it monotonically increasing or monotonically decreasing on $\left[-b,-a\right]$?

(2) Given that an even function $g\left(x\right)$ is monotonically decreasing on $\left[a,b\right]$, is it monotonically increasing or monotonically decreasing on $\left[-b,-a\right]$?

### Solution to Problem 17

By **Definition 3.2.6 (Odd Function)** and **Definition 3.2.5 (Even Function)**, use the odd-even property to transform.

(1) Take any $x_1,x_2\in\left[-b,-a\right]$ with $x_1<{}x_2$; then $-x_1>-x_2$, and $-x_1,-x_2\in\left[a,b\right]$. Since $f\left(x\right)$ is monotonically decreasing on $\left[a,b\right]$, we get $f\left(-x_1\right)<{}f\left(-x_2\right)$. By the odd-function property, $f\left(-x_1\right)=-f\left(x_1\right)$ and $f\left(-x_2\right)=-f\left(x_2\right)$, hence $-f\left(x_1\right)<-f\left(x_2\right)$, i.e. $f\left(x_1\right)>{}f\left(x_2\right)$. Therefore $f\left(x\right)$ is monotonically decreasing on $\left[-b,-a\right]$.

(2) Take any $x_1,x_2\in\left[-b,-a\right]$ with $x_1<{}x_2$; then $-x_1>-x_2$, and $-x_1,-x_2\in\left[a,b\right]$. Since $g\left(x\right)$ is monotonically decreasing on $\left[a,b\right]$, we get $g\left(-x_1\right)<{}g\left(-x_2\right)$. By the even-function property, $g\left(-x_1\right)=g\left(x_1\right)$ and $g\left(-x_2\right)=g\left(x_2\right)$, hence $g\left(x_1\right)<{}g\left(x_2\right)$. Therefore $g\left(x\right)$ is monotonically increasing on $\left[-b,-a\right]$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 18

(Review Reference Problem 3, Comprehensive Application, Problem 10) Last year the electricity price in a certain region was $0.8$ yuan/$\left(\text{kW}\cdot\text{h}\right)$, and the annual electricity consumption was $a\;\text{kW}\cdot\text{h}$. This year it is planned to lower the price to between $0.55$ yuan/$\left(\text{kW}\cdot\text{h}\right)$ and $0.75$ yuan/$\left(\text{kW}\cdot\text{h}\right)$, while users expect a price of $0.4$ yuan/$\left(\text{kW}\cdot\text{h}\right)$. It is estimated that after the price is lowered, the added electricity consumption is inversely proportional to the difference between the actual price and the users' expected price (proportionality coefficient $k$). The cost price of electricity in this region is $0.3$ yuan/$\left(\text{kW}\cdot\text{h}\right)$.

(1) Write the analytic expression for the power department's revenue $y$ (unit: yuan) as a function of the actual price $x$ (unit: yuan/$\left(\text{kW}\cdot\text{h}\right)$) after this year's price cut. (Revenue $=$ actual consumption $\times$ (actual price $-$ cost price))

(2) Let $k=0.2a$. What is the minimum price that still guarantees the power department's revenue increases by at least $20\%$ over last year?

### Solution to Problem 18

By **Definition 3.1.1 (Function)**, build the revenue-as-a-function-of-price according to the problem.

(1) The added consumption is $\frac{k}{x-0.4}$, and the actual consumption is $a+\frac{k}{x-0.4}$. The revenue is

$$
y=\left(a+\frac{k}{x-0.4}\right)\left(x-0.3\right),\quad{x}\in\left[0.55,0.75\right].
$$

(2) Last year's revenue was $a\left(0.8-0.3\right)=0.5a$. This year's revenue should increase by at least $20\%$, i.e. $y\ge0.5a\cdot1.2=0.6a$.

Substituting $k=0.2a$:

$$
\left(a+\frac{0.2a}{x-0.4}\right)\left(x-0.3\right)\ge0.6a.
$$

Dividing both sides by $a$:

$$
\left(1+\frac{0.2}{x-0.4}\right)\left(x-0.3\right)\ge0.6.
$$

Expanding: $\frac{x-0.4+0.2}{x-0.4}\cdot\left(x-0.3\right)=\frac{\left(x-0.2\right)\left(x-0.3\right)}{x-0.4}\ge0.6$.

i.e. $x^2-0.5x+0.06\ge0.6x-0.24$, rearranging to $x^2-1.1x+0.3\ge0$.

Solving gives $x\le0.5$ or $x\ge0.6$. Combined with $x\in\left[0.55,0.75\right]$, we get $x\ge0.6$. Hence the minimum price is set at $0.6$ yuan/$\left(\text{kW}\cdot\text{h}\right)$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 20

(Review Reference Problem 3, Extension and Exploration, Problem 12) Discuss the domain, range, monotonicity and odd-even property of the function $y=x-\frac{1}{x}$, and sketch its graph.

The graph: in the rectangular coordinate plane, the graph of $y=x-\frac{1}{x}$ is symmetric about the origin (an odd function) and splits into two branches: in the first quadrant, the curve increases monotonically from $-\infty$ as $x\to0^+$ to $+\infty$, with the line $y=x$ and the $y$-axis as asymptotes; in the third quadrant is the other branch symmetric about the origin, increasing monotonically from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$ as $x\to0^-$.

### Solution to Problem 20

By **Definition 3.2.6 (Odd Function)**, **Definition 3.2.1 (Monotonic Increase and Increasing Function)** and **Definition 3.2.2 (Monotonic Decrease and Decreasing Function)**.

Domain: $x\ne0$, i.e. $\left(-\infty,0\right)\cup\left(0,+\infty\right)$.

Odd-even property: $f\left(-x\right)=-x-\frac{1}{-x}=-x+\frac{1}{x}=-\left(x-\frac{1}{x}\right)=-f\left(x\right)$, hence it is an odd function.

Monotonicity: take any $x_1,x_2\in\left(0,+\infty\right)$ with $x_1<{}x_2$, then

$$
f\left(x_1\right)-f\left(x_2\right)=\left(x_1-\frac{1}{x_1}\right)-\left(x_2-\frac{1}{x_2}\right)=\left(x_1-x_2\right)+\left(\frac{1}{x_2}-\frac{1}{x_1}\right)=\left(x_1-x_2\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{x_1x_2}\right).
$$

From $x_1<{}x_2$ we get $x_1-x_2<0$, and $1+\frac{1}{x_1x_2}>0$, hence $f\left(x_1\right)-f\left(x_2\right)<0$, i.e. $f\left(x_1\right)<{}f\left(x_2\right)$, so it is monotonically increasing on $\left(0,+\infty\right)$. By the odd-function property, it is also monotonically increasing on $\left(-\infty,0\right)$.

Range: as $x\to0^+$, $y\to-\infty$; as $x\to+\infty$, $y\to+\infty$; and since the function is continuously increasing on $\left(0,+\infty\right)$, the range is $\mathbb{R}$. The graph is symmetric about the origin, increasing from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$ in the first quadrant, with $y=x$ and the $y$-axis as asymptotes. $\blacksquare$
