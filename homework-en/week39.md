> **Definition 6.1.1 (向量与数量)**
> 
> 既有大小又有方向的量叫做向量; 只有大小没有方向的量叫做数量.

> 
> 实数 lambda 与向量 a 的乘积 lambda a 是一个向量, 它的长度是 |lambda| * |a|, 方向当 lambda > 0 时与 a 相同, lambda < 0 时相反.

>

> 
> 如果 e1, e2 是同一平面内的两个不共线向量, 那么对于这一平面内的任一向量 a, 有且只有一对实数 lambda1, lambda2, 使 a = lambda1*e1 + lambda2*e2.

## Problem 1

(Review Reference Problem 6, Review and Consolidation, Problem 10) As shown in the figure, support $A$ is acted on by two forces $\boldsymbol{F}_1$ and $\boldsymbol{F}_2$. Given that $\boldsymbol{F}_1$ makes an angle $\theta$ with the horizontal, $\lvert\boldsymbol{F}_1\rvert=40\text{~N}$, $\boldsymbol{F}_2$ is horizontal, $\lvert\boldsymbol{F}_2\rvert=70\text{~N}$, and the magnitude of the resultant $\boldsymbol{F}$ of $\boldsymbol{F}_1$ and $\boldsymbol{F}_2$ is $100\text{~N}$, find $\cos\theta$ and the cosine of the angle $\beta$ between $\boldsymbol{F}$ and $\boldsymbol{F}_2$.

(Figure: support $A$ is acted on by two forces $\boldsymbol{F}_1,\boldsymbol{F}_2$; $\boldsymbol{F}_1$ points upward at angle $\theta$ to the horizontal, $\boldsymbol{F}_2$ points horizontally to the right, and their resultant $\boldsymbol{F}$ points upward at angle $\beta$ to $\boldsymbol{F}_2$.)

### Solution to Problem 1

By **Definition 6.2.11 (Dot Product)** and **Theorem 6.4.2 (Law of Cosines)**.

$\boldsymbol{F}=\boldsymbol{F}_1+\boldsymbol{F}_2$.

$\lvert\boldsymbol{F}\rvert^2=\lvert\boldsymbol{F}_1\rvert^2+\lvert\boldsymbol{F}_2\rvert^2+2\boldsymbol{F}_1\cdot\boldsymbol{F}_2$.

$10000=1600+4900+2\times40\times70\times\cos\theta$.

$3500=5600\cos\theta$. $\cos\theta=\frac{3500}{5600}=\frac{5}{8}$.

$\boldsymbol{F}\cdot\boldsymbol{F}_2=\left(\boldsymbol{F}_1+\boldsymbol{F}_2\right)\cdot\boldsymbol{F}_2=\boldsymbol{F}_1\cdot\boldsymbol{F}_2+\boldsymbol{F}_2^2=40\times70\times\frac{5}{8}+4900=1750+4900=6650$.

$\cos\beta=\frac{\boldsymbol{F}\cdot\boldsymbol{F}_2}{\lvert\boldsymbol{F}\rvert\lvert\boldsymbol{F}_2\rvert}=\frac{6650}{100\times70}=\frac{19}{20}$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 3

(Review Reference Problem 6, Review and Consolidation, Problem 12) There is a small island in the sea, with reefs within $3\text{~n~mile}$ around it. A ship sails from west to east and sights the island at $75^{\circ}$ east of north; after sailing $8\text{~n~mile}$, it sights the island at $55^{\circ}$ east of north. If the ship continues on its course, is there a danger of hitting the reef?

### Solution to Problem 3

By **Theorem 6.4.3 (Law of Sines)**.

Let the island be $P$, the initial position $A$, and the position after sailing $B$. $AB=8$. $\angle{P}AB=90^{\circ}-75^{\circ}=15^{\circ}$. $\angle{P}BA=180^{\circ}-\left(90^{\circ}-55^{\circ}\right)=145^{\circ}$. $\angle{A}PB=180^{\circ}-15^{\circ}-145^{\circ}=20^{\circ}$.

By the Law of Sines: $\frac{AP}{\sin145^{\circ}}=\frac{8}{\sin20^{\circ}}$.

$AP=\frac{8\sin145^{\circ}}{\sin20^{\circ}}=\frac{8\sin35^{\circ}}{\sin20^{\circ}}$.

The perpendicular distance from the island to the course (the east-west direction): $d=AP\sin15^{\circ}=\frac{8\sin35^{\circ}\sin15^{\circ}}{\sin20^{\circ}}\approx\frac{8\times0.574\times0.259}{0.342}\approx3.47>3$.

Hence there is no danger of hitting the reef. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 5

(Review Reference Problem 6, Comprehensive Application, Problem 14) Given that $\boldsymbol{a}$, $\boldsymbol{b}$, $\boldsymbol{c}$, $\boldsymbol{d}$ are nonzero vectors, prove the following conclusions and explain their geometric meaning.

(1) $\boldsymbol{a}\perp\boldsymbol{b}\Longleftrightarrow\lvert\boldsymbol{a}+\boldsymbol{b}\rvert=\lvert\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{b}\rvert$;

(2) If $\boldsymbol{a}+\boldsymbol{b}=\boldsymbol{c}$, $\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{b}=\boldsymbol{d}$, then $\lvert\boldsymbol{a}\rvert=\lvert\boldsymbol{b}\rvert\Longleftrightarrow\boldsymbol{c}\perp\boldsymbol{d}$.

### Solution to Problem 5

By **Common Result 6.2.13** (Properties of the Dot Product).

(1) $\lvert\boldsymbol{a}+\boldsymbol{b}\rvert^2=\boldsymbol{a}^2+2\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}+\boldsymbol{b}^2$.

$\lvert\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{b}\rvert^2=\boldsymbol{a}^2-2\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}+\boldsymbol{b}^2$.

The equality of the two is equivalent to $4\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}=0$, i.e. $\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}=0$, i.e. $\boldsymbol{a}\perp\boldsymbol{b}$. Geometric meaning: the diagonals of a rectangle are equal.

(2) $\boldsymbol{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{d}=\left(\boldsymbol{a}+\boldsymbol{b}\right)\cdot\left(\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{b}\right)=\boldsymbol{a}^2-\boldsymbol{b}^2$.

$\boldsymbol{c}\perp\boldsymbol{d}\Longleftrightarrow\boldsymbol{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{d}=0\Longleftrightarrow\boldsymbol{a}^2=\boldsymbol{b}^2\Longleftrightarrow\lvert\boldsymbol{a}\rvert=\lvert\boldsymbol{b}\rvert$. Geometric meaning: the diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular to each other. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 6

(Review Reference Problem 6, Comprehensive Application, Problem 15) Given $\triangle{P}_1P_2P_3$, the vectors $\boldsymbol{OP_1}$, $\boldsymbol{OP_2}$, $\boldsymbol{OP_3}$ satisfy $\boldsymbol{OP_1}+\boldsymbol{OP_2}+\boldsymbol{OP_3}=\boldsymbol{0}$, $\lvert\boldsymbol{OP_1}\rvert=\lvert\boldsymbol{OP_2}\rvert=\lvert\boldsymbol{OP_3}\rvert$. Prove: $\triangle{P}_1P_2P_3$ is an equilateral triangle.

### Solution to Problem 6

By **Definition 6.2.11 (Dot Product)** and **Theorem 6.4.2 (Law of Cosines)**.

Let $\lvert\boldsymbol{OP_1}\rvert=\lvert\boldsymbol{OP_2}\rvert=\lvert\boldsymbol{OP_3}\rvert=R$.

$\boldsymbol{OP_1}+\boldsymbol{OP_2}=-\boldsymbol{OP_3}$. Squaring both sides: $\boldsymbol{OP_1}^2+\boldsymbol{OP_2}^2+2\boldsymbol{OP_1}\cdot\boldsymbol{OP_2}=\boldsymbol{OP_3}^2$.

$R^2+R^2+2R^2\cos\angle{P}_1OP_2=R^2$. $\cos\angle{P}_1OP_2=-\frac{1}{2}$. $\angle{P}_1OP_2=120^{\circ}$.

Similarly $\angle{P}_2OP_3=\angle{P}_3OP_1=120^{\circ}$.

$P_1P_2^2=R^2+R^2-2R^2\cos120^{\circ}=3R^2$. $P_1P_2=R\sqrt3$.

The three sides are equal, so it is an equilateral triangle. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 7

(Review Reference Problem 6, Comprehensive Application, Problem 16) As shown in the figure, given $\boldsymbol{OA}=\boldsymbol{a}$, $\boldsymbol{OB}=\boldsymbol{b}$, the point symmetric to any point $M$ about point $A$ is $S$, and the point symmetric to $S$ about point $B$ is $N$; express the vector $\boldsymbol{MN}$ in terms of $\boldsymbol{a}$ and $\boldsymbol{b}$.

(Figure: from point $O$ two vectors $\boldsymbol{OA}=\boldsymbol{a}$ and $\boldsymbol{OB}=\boldsymbol{b}$; any point $M$, its reflection about $A$ is $S$, and the reflection of $S$ about $B$ is $N$, with $M,S,N,A,B$ arranged collinearly.)

### Solution to Problem 7

By **Common Result 6.3.6** (Midpoint Coordinate Formula) and **Definition 6.2.5 (Vector Subtraction)**.

$A$ is the midpoint of $MS$: $\boldsymbol{OA}=\frac{\boldsymbol{OM}+\boldsymbol{OS}}{2}$. $\boldsymbol{OS}=2\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{OM}$.

$B$ is the midpoint of $SN$: $\boldsymbol{OB}=\frac{\boldsymbol{OS}+\boldsymbol{ON}}{2}$. $\boldsymbol{ON}=2\boldsymbol{b}-\boldsymbol{OS}=2\boldsymbol{b}-2\boldsymbol{a}+\boldsymbol{OM}$.

$\boldsymbol{MN}=\boldsymbol{ON}-\boldsymbol{OM}=2\boldsymbol{b}-2\boldsymbol{a}+\boldsymbol{OM}-\boldsymbol{OM}=2\left(\boldsymbol{b}-\boldsymbol{a}\right)$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 8

(Review Reference Problem 6, Extension and Exploration, Problem 17) A person rides a bicycle from place $A$ eastward for $9\text{~km}$ to place $B$, then from place $B$ in the direction $30^{\circ}$ south of east for $6\text{~km}$ to place $C$, then from place $C$ in the direction $30^{\circ}$ north of east for $16\text{~km}$ to place $D$; find the displacement of this person from place $A$ to place $D$ (angle accurate to $1^{\circ}$).

### Solution to Problem 8

By **Definition 6.2.11 (Dot Product)** and **Theorem 6.4.2 (Law of Cosines)**.

Take the east direction as the $x$-axis and the north direction as the $y$-axis.

$\boldsymbol{AB}=\left(9,0\right)$.

$\boldsymbol{BC}=6\left(\sin30^{\circ},-\cos30^{\circ}\right)=\left(3,-3\sqrt3\right)$.

$\boldsymbol{CD}=16\left(\sin30^{\circ},\cos30^{\circ}\right)=\left(8,8\sqrt3\right)$.

$\boldsymbol{AD}=\boldsymbol{AB}+\boldsymbol{BC}+\boldsymbol{CD}=\left(9+3+8,0-3\sqrt3+8\sqrt3\right)=\left(20,5\sqrt3\right)$.

$\lvert\boldsymbol{AD}\rvert=\sqrt{400+75}=\sqrt{475}=5\sqrt{19}\approx21.8\text{~km}$.

Direction: $\tan\theta=\frac{5\sqrt3}{20}=\frac{\sqrt3}{4}$. $\theta\approx23^{\circ}$, i.e. $23^{\circ}$ north of east. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 9

(Review Reference Problem 6, Extension and Exploration, Problem 18) Design a method, using two observation points $C$, $D$ (where the distance between $C$ and $D$ is $d$), to measure the heading and speed of a ship.

### Solution to Problem 9

By **Theorem 6.4.2 (Law of Cosines)** and **Theorem 6.4.3 (Law of Sines)**.

Observe the ship's position simultaneously at points $C$ and $D$, and record the azimuth angles at two times $t_1$, $t_2$. Use the triangle Law of Sines to find the ship's distance to $C$ or $D$ at the two times, thereby finding the ship's displacement; dividing the displacement by the time gives the speed, and the direction of the displacement is the heading. $\blacksquare$

(4) The principal argument of the complex number $\sin40^{\circ}-\mathrm{i}\cos40^{\circ}$ is ( ).

(A) $40^{\circ}$

(B) $140^{\circ}$

(C) $220^{\circ}$

(D) $310^{\circ}$

### Solution to Problem 11

(1) By **Definition 7.2.5 (Multiplication Rule for Complex Numbers)**, $\left(a+b\mathrm{i}\right)\left(c+d\mathrm{i}\right)=\left(ac-bd\right)+\left(ad+bc\right)\mathrm{i}$. The product is a real number if and only if the imaginary part $ad+bc=0$. Therefore choose (A).

(2) By **Definition 7.2.7 (Division Rule for Complex Numbers)**, $\frac{5}{\mathrm{i}-2}=\frac{5\left(-2-\mathrm{i}\right)}{\left(\mathrm{i}-2\right)\left(-2-\mathrm{i}\right)}=\frac{-10-5\mathrm{i}}{5}=-2-\mathrm{i}$. By **Definition 7.1.10 (Conjugate Complex Number)**, its conjugate complex number is $-2+\mathrm{i}$, i.e. $\mathrm{i}-2$. Therefore choose (B).

(3) By **Definition 7.2.1 (Complex Addition Rule)** and **Definition 7.2.2 (Complex Subtraction Rule)**, $m\left(3+\mathrm{i}\right)-\left(2+\mathrm{i}\right)=\left(3m-2\right)+\left(m-1\right)\mathrm{i}$. When $\frac{2}{3}<{}m<1$, $3m-2>0$, $m-1<0$. By **Definition 7.1.8 (Geometric Meaning of a Complex Number)**, a point with positive real part and negative imaginary part lies in the fourth quadrant. Therefore choose (D).

(4) $\sin40^{\circ}-\mathrm{i}\cos40^{\circ}$. By trigonometric identities, $\sin40^{\circ}=\cos50^{\circ}$, $\cos40^{\circ}=\sin50^{\circ}$, so the original expression $=\cos50^{\circ}-\mathrm{i}\sin50^{\circ}=\cos\left(-50^{\circ}\right)+\mathrm{i}\sin\left(-50^{\circ}\right)=\cos310^{\circ}+\mathrm{i}\sin310^{\circ}$. By **Definition 7.3.3 (Principal Value of the Argument)**, the principal argument is $310^{\circ}$. Therefore choose (D). $\blacksquare$

## Problem 12

(Review Reference Problem 7, Review and Consolidation, Problem 2) Fill in the blanks.

(1) If the modulus of the complex number $z$ is $5$ and the imaginary part is $-4$, then the complex number $z=\;\_\_\;$.

(2) Given the complex number $z=1-2\mathrm{i}$, then $\frac{1}{\overline{z}}=\;\_\_\;$.

(3) The vectors corresponding to the complex numbers $-1+\mathrm{i}$ and $3-4\mathrm{i}$ are $\boldsymbol{OA}$ and $\boldsymbol{OB}$ respectively; then the complex number corresponding to the vector $\boldsymbol{BA}$ is $\;\_\_\;$.

(4) If the vector $\boldsymbol{OZ}$ corresponds to the complex number $4\mathrm{i}$, and $\boldsymbol{OZ}$ is rotated counterclockwise by $45^{\circ}$ about point $O$ and then its modulus is changed to $\sqrt{2}$ times the original to obtain the vector $\boldsymbol{OZ_1}$, then the complex number corresponding to $\boldsymbol{OZ_1}$ is $\;\_\_\;$ (expressed in algebraic form).

### Solution to Problem 12

(1) Let $z=a-4\mathrm{i}$. By **Definition 7.1.9 (Modulus of a Complex Number)**, $\lvert{z}\rvert=\sqrt{a^2+\left(-4\right)^2}=5$, so $a^2+16=25$, $a^2=9$, $a=\pm3$. Hence $z=3-4\mathrm{i}$ or $z=-3-4\mathrm{i}$.

(2) By **Definition 7.1.10 (Conjugate Complex Number)**, $\overline{z}=1+2\mathrm{i}$. By **Definition 7.2.7 (Division Rule for Complex Numbers)**, $\frac{1}{\overline{z}}=\frac{1}{1+2\mathrm{i}}=\frac{1-2\mathrm{i}}{\left(1+2\mathrm{i}\right)\left(1-2\mathrm{i}\right)}=\frac{1-2\mathrm{i}}{5}=\frac{1}{5}-\frac{2}{5}\mathrm{i}$.

(3) By **Definition 7.2.2 (Subtraction Rule for Complex Numbers)**, $\boldsymbol{BA}=\boldsymbol{OA}-\boldsymbol{OB}$. The corresponding complex number is $\left(-1+\mathrm{i}\right)-\left(3-4\mathrm{i}\right)=-4+5\mathrm{i}$.

(4) The vector $\boldsymbol{OZ}$ corresponds to the complex number $4\mathrm{i}=4\left(\cos90^{\circ}+\mathrm{i}\sin90^{\circ}\right)$. By **Common Result 7.3.7** (Geometric Meaning of Multiplication), rotate counterclockwise by $45^{\circ}$ and change the modulus to $\sqrt{2}$ times the original: the modulus is $4\sqrt{2}$, and the argument is $90^{\circ}+45^{\circ}=135^{\circ}$. Converting to algebraic form: $4\sqrt{2}\left(\cos135^{\circ}+\mathrm{i}\sin135^{\circ}\right)=4\sqrt{2}\left(-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\mathrm{i}\right)=-4+4\mathrm{i}$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 13

(Review Reference Problem 7, Review and Consolidation, Problem 3) Prove: $z\cdot\overline{z}=\lvert{z}\rvert^2=\lvert\overline{z}\rvert^2$.

### Solution to Problem 13

Let $z=a+b\mathrm{i}\,\left(a,b\in\mathbb{R}\right)$. By **Definition 7.1.10 (Conjugate Complex Number)**, $\overline{z}=a-b\mathrm{i}$.

By **Definition 7.2.5 (Multiplication Rule for Complex Numbers)**,

$$
z\cdot\overline{z}=\left(a+b\mathrm{i}\right)\left(a-b\mathrm{i}\right)=a^2-b^2\mathrm{i}^2=a^2+b^2.
$$

By **Definition 7.1.9 (Modulus of a Complex Number)**, $\lvert{z}\rvert^2=a^2+b^2$, $\lvert\overline{z}\rvert^2=a^2+\left(-b\right)^2=a^2+b^2$.

Hence $z\cdot\overline{z}=\lvert{z}\rvert^2=\lvert\overline{z}\rvert^2$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 14

(Review Reference Problem 7, Review and Consolidation, Problem 4) Given that both the complex number $z$ and $\left(z+2\right)^2-8\mathrm{i}$ are pure imaginary numbers, find $z$.

### Solution to Problem 14

By **Definition 7.1.6 (Pure Imaginary Number)**, let $z=b\mathrm{i}\,\left(b\in\mathbb{R},b\neq0\right)$.

Then $z+2=2+b\mathrm{i}$. By **Definition 7.2.5 (Multiplication Rule for Complex Numbers)**,

$$
\left(z+2\right)^2=\left(2+b\mathrm{i}\right)^2=4+4b\mathrm{i}+b^2\mathrm{i}^2=\left(4-b^2\right)+4b\mathrm{i}.
$$

Hence $\left(z+2\right)^2-8\mathrm{i}=\left(4-b^2\right)+\left(4b-8\right)\mathrm{i}$.

Since $\left(z+2\right)^2-8\mathrm{i}$ is also a pure imaginary number, the real part must be $0$ and the imaginary part not equal to $0$:

$$
4-b^2=0,
$$

giving $b=\pm2$. Also requiring $4b-8\neq0$, i.e. $b\neq2$, hence $b=-2$.

Therefore $z=-2\mathrm{i}$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 15

(2) $\left(x-3\right)\left(x-5\right)+2=0$.

### Solution to Problem 15

(1) From $4x^2+9=0$, we get $x^2=-\frac{9}{4}$. Since $\left(\frac{3}{2}\mathrm{i}\right)^2=\left(-\frac{3}{2}\mathrm{i}\right)^2=-\frac{9}{4}$, we have $x=\pm\frac{3}{2}\mathrm{i}$.

x=\frac{8\pm\sqrt{4}\,\mathrm{i}}{2}=\frac{8\pm2\mathrm{i}}{2}=4\pm\mathrm{i}.
$$

i.e. $x=4+\mathrm{i}$ or $x=4-\mathrm{i}$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 16

(Review Reference Problem 7, Comprehensive Application, Problem 6) Given $z_1=5+10\mathrm{i}$, $z_2=3-4\mathrm{i}$, $\frac{1}{z}=\frac{1}{z_1}+\frac{1}{z_2}$, find $z$.

### Solution to Problem 16

By **Definition 7.2.7 (Division Rule for Complex Numbers)**, first find $\frac{1}{z_1}$ and $\frac{1}{z_2}$.

$$
\frac{1}{z_1}=\frac{1}{5+10\mathrm{i}}=\frac{5-10\mathrm{i}}{\left(5+10\mathrm{i}\right)\left(5-10\mathrm{i}\right)}=\frac{5-10\mathrm{i}}{25+100}=\frac{5-10\mathrm{i}}{125}=\frac{1}{25}-\frac{2}{25}\mathrm{i}.
$$

$$
\frac{1}{z_2}=\frac{1}{3-4\mathrm{i}}=\frac{3+4\mathrm{i}}{\left(3-4\mathrm{i}\right)\left(3+4\mathrm{i}\right)}=\frac{3+4\mathrm{i}}{9+16}=\frac{3+4\mathrm{i}}{25}=\frac{3}{25}+\frac{4}{25}\mathrm{i}.
$$

$$
\frac{1}{z}=\frac{1}{z_1}+\frac{1}{z_2}=\frac{1+3}{25}+\frac{-2+4}{25}\mathrm{i}=\frac{4}{25}+\frac{2}{25}\mathrm{i}.
$$

Hence

$$
z=\frac{1}{\frac{4+2\mathrm{i}}{25}}=\frac{25}{4+2\mathrm{i}}=\frac{25\left(4-2\mathrm{i}\right)}{\left(4+2\mathrm{i}\right)\left(4-2\mathrm{i}\right)}=\frac{100-50\mathrm{i}}{16+4}=\frac{100-50\mathrm{i}}{20}=5-\frac{5}{2}\mathrm{i}.
$$

$\blacksquare$

## Problem 17

(Review Reference Problem 7, Comprehensive Application, Problem 7) Given $\left(1+2\mathrm{i}\right)\overline{z}=4+3\mathrm{i}$, find $z$ and $\frac{z}{\overline{z}}$.

### Solution to Problem 17

By **Definition 7.2.7 (Division Rule for Complex Numbers)**,

$$
\overline{z}=\frac{4+3\mathrm{i}}{1+2\mathrm{i}}=\frac{\left(4+3\mathrm{i}\right)\left(1-2\mathrm{i}\right)}{\left(1+2\mathrm{i}\right)\left(1-2\mathrm{i}\right)}=\frac{4-8\mathrm{i}+3\mathrm{i}-6\mathrm{i}^2}{1+4}=\frac{10-5\mathrm{i}}{5}=2-\mathrm{i}.

Then find $\frac{z}{\overline{z}}=\frac{2+\mathrm{i}}{2-\mathrm{i}}=\frac{\left(2+\mathrm{i}\right)\left(2+\mathrm{i}\right)}{\left(2-\mathrm{i}\right)\left(2+\mathrm{i}\right)}=\frac{4+4\mathrm{i}+\mathrm{i}^2}{4+1}=\frac{3+4\mathrm{i}}{5}=\frac{3}{5}+\frac{4}{5}\mathrm{i}$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 18

(Review Reference Problem 7, Comprehensive Application, Problem 8) (1) Find the values of $\mathrm{i}^1$, $\mathrm{i}^2$, $\mathrm{i}^3$, $\mathrm{i}^4$, $\mathrm{i}^5$, $\mathrm{i}^6$, $\mathrm{i}^7$, $\mathrm{i}^8$;

(2) From (1), conjecture what pattern the values of $\mathrm{i}^n\,\left(n\in\mathbb{N}^*\right)$ follow, and express this pattern as a formula.

### Solution to Problem 18

By **Definition 7.1.1 (Imaginary Unit)**, $\mathrm{i}^2=-1$.

(1) Compute one by one:

(a) $\mathrm{i}^1=\mathrm{i}$;

(b) $\mathrm{i}^2=-1$;

(c) $\mathrm{i}^3=\mathrm{i}^2\cdot\mathrm{i}=-\mathrm{i}$;

(d) $\mathrm{i}^4=\mathrm{i}^3\cdot\mathrm{i}=\left(-\mathrm{i}\right)\cdot\mathrm{i}=1$;

(g) $\mathrm{i}^7=\mathrm{i}^6\cdot\mathrm{i}=-\mathrm{i}$;

## Problem 19

(Review Reference Problem 7, Extension and Exploration, Problem 9) Given the complex numbers $z_1=m+\left(4-m^2\right)\mathrm{i}\,\left(m\in\mathbb{R}\right)$, $z_2=2\cos\theta+\left(\lambda+3\sin\theta\right)\mathrm{i}\,\left(\lambda,\theta\in\mathbb{R}\right)$, and $z_1=z_2$, find the range of values of $\lambda$.

### Solution to Problem 19

By **Definition 7.1.5 (Equality of Complex Numbers)**, $z_1=z_2$ if and only if the real parts are equal and the imaginary parts are equal.

Real parts equal: $m=2\cos\theta$.

Imaginary parts equal: $4-m^2=\lambda+3\sin\theta$.

Substituting $m=2\cos\theta$ into the imaginary-part equation, we get

$$
4-4\cos^2\theta=\lambda+3\sin\theta.
$$

i.e.

$$
\lambda=4\sin^2\theta-3\sin\theta.
$$

Let $t=\sin\theta$; then $t\in\left[-1,1\right]$, $\lambda=4t^2-3t$.

This is a quadratic function in $t$, opening upward, with axis of symmetry $t=\frac{3}{8}\in\left[-1,1\right]$. The minimum is at $t=\frac{3}{8}$: $\lambda_{\min}=4\cdot\frac{9}{64}-3\cdot\frac{3}{8}=\frac{9}{16}-\frac{9}{8}=-\frac{9}{16}$.

The maximum is taken at an endpoint: when $t=-1$, $\lambda=4+3=7$; when $t=1$, $\lambda=4-3=1$. Hence $\lambda_{\max}=7$.

Therefore the range of values of $\lambda$ is $\left[-\frac{9}{16},7\right]$. $\blacksquare$

## Problem 20

(Review Reference Problem 7, Extension and Exploration, Problem 10) In the upper half of the complex plane there is a rhombus $OABC$, $\angle{A}OC=120^{\circ}$, and the complex number corresponding to point $A$ is $2+\mathrm{i}$; find the complex numbers corresponding to the other two vertices $B$ and $C$.

### Solution to Problem 20

By **Common Result 7.3.7** (Geometric Meaning of Complex Multiplication), rotating the vector $\boldsymbol{OA}$ by the angle gives the vector $\boldsymbol{OC}$.

In the rhombus $OABC$, $O$ is the origin, $\angle{A}OC=120^{\circ}$, and $\lvert\boldsymbol{OA}\rvert=\lvert\boldsymbol{OC}\rvert$ (the sides of a rhombus are equal). Point $A$ corresponds to the complex number $z_A=2+\mathrm{i}$.

Rotate $\boldsymbol{OA}$ counterclockwise by $120^{\circ}$ about point $O$ to obtain $\boldsymbol{OC}$ (since it is in the upper half-plane):

$$
z_C=\left(2+\mathrm{i}\right)\left(\cos120^{\circ}+\mathrm{i}\sin120^{\circ}\right)=\left(2+\mathrm{i}\right)\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\mathrm{i}\right).
$$

Expanding:

$$
z_C=2\cdot\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)+2\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\mathrm{i}+\mathrm{i}\cdot\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\mathrm{i}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\mathrm{i}=-1+\sqrt{3}\mathrm{i}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{i}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}=-\frac{2+\sqrt{3}}{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{3}-1}{2}\mathrm{i}.
$$

The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other, $\boldsymbol{OB}=\boldsymbol{OA}+\boldsymbol{OC}$, so

$$
z_B=z_A+z_C=\left(2+\mathrm{i}\right)+\left(-\frac{2+\sqrt{3}}{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{3}-1}{2}\mathrm{i}\right)=\frac{2-\sqrt{3}}{2}+\frac{1+2\sqrt{3}}{2}\mathrm{i}.
$$

Therefore the complex number corresponding to point $B$ is $\frac{2-\sqrt{3}}{2}+\frac{1+2\sqrt{3}}{2}\mathrm{i}$, and the complex number corresponding to point $C$ is $-\frac{2+\sqrt{3}}{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{3}-1}{2}\mathrm{i}$. $\blacksquare$
